PRINCIPLES OVER PROGRAMS.

Train with intent.

How to get the most from three sessions a week and one kettlebell.

THE APPROACH

Strength is a practice,
not a sprint.

Three full-body sessions a week, each built around the six things a kettlebell does best: squat, hinge, press, pull, carry, and everything your core does while you hold one. Show up, move well, add a little each week.

01 · EFFORT

Leave reps in the tank

End every set with one to three clean reps still available. The last rep should look like the first. Grinding teaches bad form; crisp reps build strength that lasts.

02 · PROGRESSION

Reps first, then the bell

Kettlebells jump in 4 kg steps, so progress through reps. When you hit the top of a range on every set, take the next bell up and restart at the bottom of the range.

03 · RECOVERY

Grow between sessions

Train on non-consecutive days, sleep seven to nine hours, and eat enough protein — around 1.6 to 2.2 g per kilogram of bodyweight. Every six to eight weeks, take a lighter week.

Warm up before every session

About five minutes · two easy rounds
01Halo

Eight each direction. Circle the bell around your head to open the shoulders.

02Goblet squat pry

Five reps, three seconds in the bottom. Press the knees out with the elbows.

03Hip hinge

Ten reps with a light bell. Hips back, flat back, feel the hamstrings.

04Easy swings

Ten light swings to rehearse the hinge and wake the whole body up.

One kettlebell is enough

Most people start pressing with 8–12 kg and squatting, rowing and hinging with 12–16 kg. If you are between sizes, take the lighter one — clean reps above the range beat heavy reps below it. A second matching bell doubles the load on squats, presses and carries once the top of a range feels easy.
